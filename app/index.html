Step 4 — Build Your Paycheck Budget

Split your plan across your actual paydays. Bills are assigned to the paycheck before their due date — never the one after. Every paycheck must balance: Assigned total = that paycheck's take-home. The order matters: essentials → minimums → target debt/savings → lifestyle. If the money runs out before lifestyle, that's information, not failure. (§6)

Step 8 — Sinking Funds: Stop “Surprise” Expenses Ruining the Month

Car repairs, holidays, and annual insurance aren't emergencies — they're predictable. A sinking fund = expected cost ÷ months until it's due, saved a little every paycheck. Keep the money in a separate savings account if you can — money you can see in your checking account gets spent. (§10, §16)

The Rules of This System (read once, use forever — §2)

1Budget by paycheck, not by month. A monthly budget fails when rent is due on the 1st but you get paid on the 5th and the 19th. Every paycheck gets a job before it arrives.
2Every dollar gets an assignment. Income − assignments = $0 on paper. That doesn't mean spend it all — “savings” and “debt extra” are assignments.
3Minimums first, extra money second. Every debt gets its minimum paid on time, every time. Extra money attacks one target debt.
4Sinking funds beat willpower. Car repairs, holidays, and annual insurance are not emergencies — they're predictable. Save for them monthly in small amounts.
5Review beats perfection. A budget you check for 15 minutes a week beats a perfect budget you abandon in week two.

Quick-Reference Formulas (§21)

Monthly interest on a debt

interest = balance × (APR ÷ 100) ÷ 12

SaveWare shows this live for every debt on the Debts tab.

Totals

total debt = sum of all balances  ·  total minimums = sum of all minimum payments

Months to clear a debt at a fixed payment (approx.)

months = −LN(1 − monthly interest ÷ payment) ÷ LN(1 + (APR ÷ 100) ÷ 12)

Use the payment you'll actually make (the minimum, or minimum + extra for your target debt). The guard matters: if the payment doesn't exceed the month's interest, the balance would never fall — SaveWare warns you with “minimum doesn't cover the interest” instead of a meaningless number. A 0% APR debt is the simple case: months = balance ÷ payment. In the planner, SaveWare doesn't rely on this formula alone — it runs a full month-by-month simulation, rolling each cleared debt's payment into the next target, which is why its timelines match the workbook's worked example.

Sinking fund per month

per month = expected cost ÷ months until due

The income check

sum of all assignments = total take-home

A paycheck budget is balanced when its assignments add up to exactly that paycheck's take-home pay. A gap either way is a decision to make on purpose — in the budget, not a surprise at month-end. That's why every paycheck card in SaveWare shows ✓ Balanced or ⚠ Assigned ≠ paycheck.
